Prototype · Round 2

BuffPath

Round 1 was one flat daily deck. This version sorts every posting into three tiers by how contested it is, reads the real posting for what the employer actually screens on, and shows you your own application the way the recruiter will see it.

The three tiers
Priority
The contested roles — hundreds of applicants for a handful of seats. You can't fast-apply. You pick one requirement and write a line about it, and that line is the first thing the recruiter reads.
Basic
The middle of the market, and most of the volume. This is where the swipe deck lives, because triage is the right verb here.
Last Call
Closing inside three days and you still haven't applied. Nothing is here because it's bad — it's here because you're about to lose it.
What extraction reads
  • Every requirement lands in one of three states against your resume: met, met but you worded it differently, or genuinely missing.
  • The wording state is the useful one. "You have this, you just called it something else" is a fifteen-second fix; a real gap is a prep step.
  • Extraction is fallible, so every line can be dismissed. A machine reading of a posting never overrides yours.
  • Rewording never happens silently. The app asks what you actually did before it touches your resume.
Jump to a screen
Open questions
  • Applicant volume is what sorts Priority from Basic. Public boards rarely publish it, so this leans on CU's own hiring history plus employer-reported counts.
  • Scraping Handshake directly will hit terms-of-service limits. The tiers work on public boards regardless, but the requirement extraction is thinner without it.
  • The recruiter preview is a claim about what employers see. It should be verified with CU Career Services before it ships as a promise.
