# Open questions

Unresolved, each with what would resolve it. Ordered by how much rides on the answer.

## Q1 · Will students actually write the Priority line?

Everything distinctive rests on this (D4). If the line reads as homework and students look for a way around it, BuffPath is a nicer job board.

**How to resolve.** Fifteen students, the prototype on a phone, no explanation. Watch whether anyone writes a real sentence, tries to skip, or types filler. This is cheap and answers more than any further design work.

## Q2 · Is applicant volume obtainable at scale?

D2 makes contest level the sorting axis. Public boards rarely publish it.

**How to resolve.** Ask Career Services what Handshake exposes. In parallel, test whether proxies (named program, company size, posting age) predict volume well enough on postings where the number is known.

## Q3 · Does the reworded state change behavior?

The met / reworded / missing split is only worth its complexity if the middle state sends someone to their resume (D6).

**How to resolve.** Same session as Q1. Watch whether anyone taps "Match their wording," and whether the follow-up question feels like help or an obstacle.

## Q4 · Does a written line change how recruiters read an applicant?

The recruiter preview (D16) is a claim about employer behavior, currently unverified.

**How to resolve.** Two or three recruiters who hire CU interns. Show them the card. Ask whether they would read it, and whether it would change the order they work through a stack.

## Q5 · Is one app right for first-years and juniors?

D13 is provisional. A first-year sees mostly missing requirements, which may discourage rather than inform.

**How to resolve.** Run Q1's session with a mix of class years and compare reactions to the same posting.

## Q6 · How reliable is extraction on real postings?

**How to resolve.** Twenty real postings, parsed by hand, scored against a student's actual resume. If the error rate is high enough that dismissing becomes routine, the feature teaches distrust.

## Q7 · Who maintains this after graduation?

Campus tools die on this question more than any other. Not a design question, but it determines whether the institutional dependency is a moat or a ceiling.

## Q8 · What counts as success?

Installs, applications submitted, or internships accepted. Only the last matters and it is the hardest to measure — it arrives months after the behavior that caused it, and the app cannot see it without asking.
