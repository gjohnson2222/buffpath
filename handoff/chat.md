I'm working on **BuffPath**, an internship-finding mobile app for CU Boulder students. It's at interactive-prototype stage: no backend, no real data, no institutional partnership.

## The short version

Every posting sorts into three tiers by how contested it is. Priority roles have hundreds of applicants for a handful of seats, and applying requires writing one line about one specific requirement rather than fast-applying. Basic is the volume, triaged in a deck. Last Call is closing inside three days and unapplied. Underneath, the app reads posting text and sorts each requirement into met, worded-differently, or missing against the student's actual resume.

## What I know is hard

**Permission, not technology.** Handshake postings, applicant volume, status sync, CU hiring history, and the Leeds alumni pool all sit behind CU Career Services and Leeds Alumni Relations. None is available to a student project by default.

**The differentiator is untested.** Everything distinctive rests on students being willing to write one honest line for a contested role. If that reads as homework, this is a nicer job board.

**Seasonality.** Internship recruiting runs in windows — roughly two real chances a year to test with students who care.

## What I'd find useful

Pressure on the assumptions rather than encouragement. Specifically: whether the contest-based tiering holds up if applicant volume proves unobtainable, whether comment-to-apply survives contact with a student in a hurry, and what the smallest honest version of this is that could ship in one recruiting season.

Please don't restate the design back to me — I know it. Argue with it.
